# Brick Sessions — IRONMAN Belgium 2027

> **What is a brick?** A bike-to-run session where you start running within minutes of finishing a bike ride — simulating the T2 transition and the challenging feeling of "dead legs" at the start of the marathon.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Ironman marathon is the hardest part.** Your legs will feel like concrete for the first 10–15 minutes of the run no matter how fit you are. Brick training teaches your neuromuscular system to transition quickly and your mind to stay patient during that initial discomfort.

---

## Why Bricks Feel Hard (And Why That's the Point)

When you step off the bike, your leg muscles have been in a fixed cycling position for hours. Blood pooling, muscle fibre recruitment patterns, and altered proprioception make running feel unnatural. Regular brick training:

1. **Accelerates neuromuscular adaptation** — your legs learn to switch modes faster
2. **Builds mental resilience** — you know what the feeling is, so it doesn't panic you
3. **Tests your fueling** — does your nutrition hold up when you transition to running?
4. **Builds run-specific fatigue tolerance** — running on tired legs is a different stimulus to fresh running

*Source: This is well-established in triathlon coaching — TrainingPeaks, British Triathlon Federation, and IRONMAN official training guides all emphasise brick sessions as irreplaceable preparation.*

## Session Library

### 🧱 Session BR1 — Introduction Brick (Weeks 18–19)

**Bike: 90 min | Run: 20 min | Total: ~1:55 h**

```
BIKE PHASE:
  Warm-up: 10 min easy spin
  Main: 70 min Z2 aerobic, cadence 88–92 rpm
  Final 10 min: slightly increase cadence to 95 rpm to prepare legs for running

TRANSITION (T2 practice):
  Stop bike, rack it (or lean it)
  Change shoes — time yourself (aim for under 3 min, faster as practice continues)
  Start GPS run timer
  
RUN PHASE:
  First 5 min: DO NOT check pace. Feel the strange heaviness — it is normal.
  Acknowledge it without panic: "This is what brick legs feel like. They pass."
  
  Minutes 5–20: Settle into Z2 run
  If legs feel okay after 15 min, note how long the transition discomfort lasted
  
  Cool-down: walk 3 min after the run
```

**After the session — notes to record:**
- How long before legs felt "normal" on the run?
- Heart rate at start of run vs. 10 min in?
- Any cramping, GI issues?

### 🧱 Session BR2 — Standard Brick (Weeks 22–40)

**Bike: 2:30–3:00 h | Run: 30–35 min | Total: ~3:00–3:30 h**

```
BIKE PHASE:
  Warm-up: 15 min easy
  Main: sustained Z2 aerobic — race effort discipline
  Fuel: every 25 min (gel/bar + fluids)
  Final 5 min: increase cadence to 95+ rpm to "spin out" the legs

TRANSITION:
  Practice T2 routine exactly as on race day
  Helmet off → shoes change → run shoes on → go
  Target under 5 min (race day, with gear bags and tent, allow 8 min)

RUN PHASE:
  First 5 min: patient, let heart rate settle
  Main 20–25 min: Z2 run — use this to practice race pacing
  Last 5 min: push slightly to Z3 to build run-off-bike confidence
  
  Fuel: take a gel at the start of the run (simulating race aid station)
```

### 🧱 Session BR3 — Race Simulation Day (Week 43)

**Bike: 4:30 h | Run: 60–90 min | Total: ~5:30–6:00 h**
**This is your most important preparation session of the entire plan.**

```
PREPARATION (night before):
  Lay out all gear exactly as race day: helmet, race kit, shoes, nutrition
  Prepare nutrition: count and bag your gels/bars for the bike AND run
  Set alarm to simulate race day timing

BIKE PHASE (4:30 h):
  Warm-up: 15 min easy
  First 30 min: deliberately restrained — simulate leaving T1 with swim legs
  Main 3:15 h: sustained Z2 at race effort
  Fuel: practise your complete race nutrition plan:
    - Gel or bar every 20–25 min
    - Drink ~500ml per hour (water/sports drink)
    - Total: ~280–320g carbohydrate over 4 hours
  
  Note at each hour:
    - How is your power/pace holding? Drifting upward means going too hard.
    - Any GI discomfort?

TRANSITION:
  Full T2 routine in race kit
  
RUN PHASE (60–90 min):
  This is the critical learning session.
  First 10 min: walk-run if needed, legs will be heavy
  Main 60–70 min: settle into race marathon effort (Z2 for run)
  Final 10 min: push to what 40 km of race day would feel like
  
  Fuel: gel at min 1 and every 20 min thereafter
  Walk 2 min every 3 km as planned for race day
  
AFTER:
  Eat a proper meal within 30 min (carbs + protein)
  Note every detail — fueling, pacing, what was hard, what was easy
  This session informs every race-day decision
```

## Transition Practice Guide

> Practice makes T2 automatic. Fumbling in T2 costs minutes.

### T2 Sequence (Bike to Run)
1. Dismount bike (practice clip-in/clip-out transitions)
2. Rack or hand off bike to volunteers
3. Remove helmet (after racking — this is mandatory in IRONMAN)
4. Open run gear bag
5. Change to run shoes (elastic laces recommended for speed)
6. Race number belt on (if not already on)
7. Hat/sunglasses if used
8. Grab run nutrition
9. Go

**Race day T2 target time:** 6–8 minutes (first Ironman — not worth rushing)

### Practicing the transition
- Set up in your garden/hallway occasionally — walk through the steps
- From Phase 3 onwards: do your brick sessions in your actual race kit

## Mental Strategy for the Marathon Start

> This applies on race day and should be practised in every brick run.

**What you will feel after getting off the bike:**
- Legs feel heavy, stiff, wooden — normal
- Pace feels slow even at a hard effort — normal
- Mild nausea or stomach settling required — common

**What to do:**
1. Start walking for the first 60–90 seconds of the run — let blood redistribute
2. Cue yourself: *"The legs always come around. Stay patient."*
3. Don't look at your pace for the first 8 minutes
4. Focus on form: upright, relaxed arms, short steps, high cadence

**What to avoid:**
- Running hard immediately off the bike (you will collapse later)
- Panicking about your pace or comparing to others
- Skipping aid stations because you "feel fine"
