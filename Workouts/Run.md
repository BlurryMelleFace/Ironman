# Run Workouts — IRONMAN Belgium 2027

> **Race run:** 42.2 km marathon · Target: ~5:30–6:00 h · Pace ~7:50–8:30/km
> **Your starting point:** 10 km comfortable (~6:00–7:00/km)

> [!WARNING]
> **The Ironman marathon is run off a 3.8 km swim and 180 km bike.** Your training run paces are irrelevant unless your legs are fatigued first. Brick sessions (see [[Brick]]) are what prepare you for this. Never compare your fresh training run pace to your goal race pace.

## Run Intensity Reference

| Zone | Description | Talk Test | Rough pace guidance* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 | Walk/Recovery jog | Full easy conversation | Slower than easy |
| Z2 | Aerobic base | Full comfortable sentences | Easy, never breathless |
| Z3 | Tempo | Short phrases | Comfortably hard |
| Z4 | Threshold | 2–3 words | Hard and controlled |
| Z5 | VO2max intervals | Cannot speak | Very hard, short |

*Pace guidance will depend on your fitness and conditions. Use the talk test, not a pace target, especially in Phase 1.

> [!TIP]
> **Your race marathon pace will likely be 7:50–8:30/km.** This is *much* slower than your fresh 10 km pace. In training, your long runs at Z2 will naturally settle somewhere around this pace — that's healthy. Don't chase pace on long runs.

## Key Running Principles for Ironman Training

### 1. Running economy over speed
At 1.91 m and 91 kg, your priority is **efficient form** that holds up under fatigue — not raw speed. Focus on:
- **Cadence:** Aim for 170–180 steps/minute (count one foot for 30 seconds × 2). Higher cadence = shorter ground contact = less impact.
- **Posture:** Slight forward lean from the ankles (not the waist). Avoid sitting back.
- **Arm swing:** Relaxed elbows at ~90°, forward-backward (not across body).
- **Foot strike:** Avoid heavy heel striking. Land under your hips, not in front.

### 2. Easy days must be easy
Zone 2 runs should feel almost embarrassingly slow. If you're ever breathless, slow down. Run-walk is fine and encouraged in Phase 1.

### 3. Respect the Achilles and plantar fascia
At your height and weight, run volume should increase cautiously. A conservative 10% weekly increase rule applies especially to long run duration. Calf strength work in the gym is your best protection.

## Session Library

### 🏃 Session R1 — Easy Aerobic Run (All phases)

**Duration:** 35–70 min | **Intensity:** Z2 | **Use:** All mid-week easy runs

```
Warm-up (included — first 10 min):
  Walk 2 min → slow jog 8 min, body warming up

Main Set:
  Run at Z2 — full comfortable conversation
  If your heart rate climbs above Z2, slow down or briefly walk
  Do NOT try to "hold pace" — pace follows fitness

Dynamic strides (optional, Phase 2+):
  End with 4 × 20 sec accelerations at stride pace (not sprint)
  Walk 45 sec between each
  Purpose: neuromuscular sharpness without fatigue

Cool-down:
  5 min walk + stretch (calves, hip flexors, quads)
```

---

### 🏃 Session R2 — Long Run (All phases)

**Duration:** 55 min – 3:00 h | **Intensity:** Z2 entirely | **Use:** Sunday long run

> This is your most important run session each week.

```
Warm-up (included — first 15 min):
  Deliberately slow — 10–15% slower than your Z2 pace

Main Set:
  Sustained Z2 running
  Walk breaks are permitted and encouraged if heart rate rises in heat or fatigue
  
  Fueling (from Phase 2 long runs >90 min):
  - Carry a gel or sports drink
  - Fuel every 30–40 min
  - Practice what you plan to use on race day

Cool-down (last 10 min):
  Easy jog → walk → stretch
  Priority stretches: hip flexors, calves, glutes
```

**Long run progression:**

| Phase | Target long run | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 end | 1:30 h | Build slowly, no soreness for >48 h |
| Phase 2 end | 2:30 h | Begin fueling practice |
| Phase 3 | Maintain 1:45–2:00 h | Don't build further during exam phase |
| Phase 4 peak | 2:45–3:00 h | Highest long run — do NOT exceed 3 h |
| Taper | 1:30 → 40 min | Reduce volume, maintain feel |

### 🏃 Session R3 — Tempo Run (Phase 2 onwards)

**Duration:** 50–65 min | **Intensity:** Z2 with Z4 tempo blocks | **Use:** 1× per week in Phase 2–4

> One of your 20% hard sessions. This develops your lactate threshold and makes Z2 feel easier.

```
Warm-up (15 min):
  Easy jog Z1–Z2, then 4 × 20 sec strides

Main Set (choose one):
  Option A — Cruise Intervals:
    4 × 8 min at Z4 (hard, few words only), 3 min Z2 jog recovery
  
  Option B — Tempo Run:
    20–30 min continuous at Z3–Z4 (comfortably hard, short phrases)
  
  Option C — Progression Run:
    First 15 min Z2 → middle 10 min Z3 → last 10 min Z4

Cool-down (10 min):
  Easy jog + stretching
```

---

### 🏃 Session R4 — Interval Run (Phase 4 only)

**Duration:** 55–65 min | **Intensity:** Z2 with Z5 intervals | **Use:** Phase 4, 1×/week

```
Warm-up (15 min):
  Easy jog + dynamic mobility (leg swings, high knees, A-skips)

Main Set:
  6–8 × 3 min at Z5 (hard — cannot speak), 2 min easy jog recovery
  Focus: maintain form when fatigued — don't let your posture collapse

Cool-down (15 min):
  Easy jog — let heart rate fully recover before stopping
```

### 🏃 Session R5 — Brick Run (off the bike)

> Full details in [[Brick]]. Brief guide here:

```
After a bike session:
  Change shoes, start running within 2–3 min of getting off the bike
  
  First 5 min: legs will feel strange and heavy — this is normal
  Settle into Z2 run pace — do NOT chase pace in first 5 min
  
  Duration:
  - Phase 2 bricks: 15–30 min
  - Phase 3 bricks: 25–40 min
  - Phase 4 bricks: 40–70 min
```

## Race Run Strategy

### The walk-run approach *(highly recommended for first Ironman)*

Elite coaches and experienced Ironman athletes consistently recommend a planned walk-run strategy for first-timers. Walking aid stations (1–2 min every 2–3 km) does not slow you down significantly but dramatically improves ability to maintain pace in the second half of the marathon.

**Race day plan:**
- First 5 km: deliberately conservative, feel like you're holding back
- Walk every aid station (approximately every 2.5 km) — drink/eat while walking
- If you feel good at 30 km: that's when you can run the aid stations
- Never try to "make up time" in the first half of the marathon

### Expected marathon pacing

| Section | Strategy | Expected pace |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 km | Held back, settle from T2 | 8:30/km |
| 10–25 km | Find rhythm, walk aid stations | 8:00–8:30/km |
| 25–35 km | This is the hard part. Stay patient | 8:30–9:00/km |
| 35–42.2 km | Whatever you have left | Vary |

**Finish time at these paces:** ~5:45–6:00 h for the marathon → total ~13:15–14:00 with transitions. This is your target range.
