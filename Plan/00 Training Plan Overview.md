## Training Philosophy

### 80/20 Polarised Approach *(established coaching consensus)*

Research by sports scientist Stephen Seiler and applied by TrainingPeaks coaches consistently shows elite and amateur endurance athletes benefit most from spending approximately:
- **~80% of training time at low intensity (Zone 2)** — fully conversational, aerobic fat-burning
- **~20% at high intensity (Zone 4–5)** — clearly hard, interval work

**The "Grey Zone" trap:** Most self-coached athletes accidentally spend too much time at moderate intensity (Zone 3 — harder than easy, not hard enough to drive adaptation). This plan deliberately avoids this.

### Zone Definitions (Talk Test primary, HR secondary)

| Zone | Name | Talk Test | Est. HR (% max) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 | Recovery | Full sentences, barely breathing harder | <60% | Active recovery only |
| Z2 | Aerobic Base | **Full comfortable sentences** | 60–72% | 80% of all training |
| Z3 | Tempo | Short sentences, noticeably breathless | 73–80% | Limited — avoid unintentionally |
| Z4 | Threshold | Few words only | 81–89% | Interval work, key sessions |
| Z5 | VO2max | Cannot speak | >90% | Rare, specific intervals only |

> [!NOTE]
> **Finding your max HR:** A practical method is to run hard uphill for 3–4 minutes as fast as you can. Your peak HR in the final 30 seconds is close to max. Alternatively use 220 − age as a rough starting estimate, then adjust with real data.

### 3:1 Recovery Cycle *(established principle)*

Every 4th week is a recovery week — volume drops 35–40%, intensity is maintained. This is non-negotiable. Adaptation happens during recovery, not during hard training.
