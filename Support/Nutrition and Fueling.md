## The Fourth Discipline

Nutrition is not an afterthought — it is the fourth discipline of triathlon. Ironman athletes who neglect race fueling consistently underperform or DNF. Your gut needs to be **trained** just like your legs.

> [!WARNING]
> **Never try new nutrition products on race day.** Every gel, bar, and drink you plan to use on September 5 must be tested extensively in training — ideally in brick sessions and long rides.

---

## Understanding Energy Demands

At your weight (~91 kg) and finishing in 12–14 hours, your total caloric expenditure will be approximately:

| Segment | Duration | Est. Expenditure |
|---|---|---|
| Swim | ~1:20 h | ~600–700 kcal |
| Bike | ~5:30–6:00 h | ~3,500–4,000 kcal |
| Run | ~5:30–6:00 h | ~3,500–4,000 kcal |
| **Total** | **~12:30–13:30 h** | **~7,500–8,700 kcal** |

You cannot replace all of this — your fat stores fill the gap. The goal is to **avoid glycogen depletion (bonking)** by supplying enough carbohydrate to keep your muscles and brain functioning.

---

## Carbohydrate Targets *(Jeukendrup, 2014 — established evidence)*

| Effort Duration | Target carbs/hour |
|---|---|
| <60 min | Not needed |
| 1–2 hours | 30–60 g/hr |
| 2–3 hours | 60–90 g/hr |
| >3 hours (race) | **60–90 g/hr on bike · 40–60 g/hr on run** |

**Key principle:** Use a glucose + fructose blend (most commercial gels/bars do this). Mixing two carbohydrate types uses different gut absorption pathways, allowing higher total intake without GI distress.

| Training Phase | Action |

## Race Day Fueling Plan

### Pre-Race

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| Night before | High carbohydrate dinner (pasta, rice, potatoes). Moderate portion — don't overeat. |
| Race morning (3 h before) | Breakfast: 80–100 g carbs. Example: 2 bagels with honey + banana + sports drink. |
| Race morning (1 h before) | 500ml sports drink or diluted juice. No solid food. |
| 15 min before swim | 200ml water |

### Swim (no fueling during swim)

Carry nothing. Focus on race start and sighting. Blood in working muscles means gut is not ready anyway.

### T1 (Swim → Bike transition)

Take your first gel here if you have time (many athletes take one in T1 bag). Don't rush — eating while panicked in transition causes GI problems.

### Bike Fueling Plan

> Start fueling at 15–20 min into the bike — not immediately.

| Target | Amount |
|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | 70–80 g per hour |
| Fluid | 500–700 ml per hour (more in heat) |
| Sodium | 500–800 mg per hour (use electrolyte tabs/drink) |
| Frequency | Fuel every 20–25 min — set a watch alert |

**Example bike nutrition (per hour):**
- 2 × energy gels (22g carbs each) = 44g
- 1 energy bar or chew (30–35g carbs) = 30–35g
- **Total: ~74–79g ✓**

**Fluids:** Alternate between water and sports drink at aid stations. Never just water alone for extended periods (hyponatraemia risk).

### T2 (Bike → Run transition)

Take a gel at the start of the run if feeling low. Eat something small during T2 if your stomach is settled.

### Run Fueling Plan

Running makes digestion harder — mechanical impact reduces gut blood flow.

| Target | Amount |
|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | 40–60 g per hour |
| Fluid | 400–600 ml per hour (sip at each aid station) |
| Sodium | 400–600 mg per hour |
| Frequency | Every aid station (walk the station, eat/drink) |

**Example run nutrition (per hour):**
- 1–2 gels per hour (22–44g carbs)
- Cola at aid stations from km 25 onwards — caffeine + fast sugar, very effective late-race
- Chicken broth at aid stations if available — sodium + comfort

> [!TIP]
> **Cola is your friend in the final 15 km.** Many athletes who are struggling find that IRONMAN-provided cola at late aid stations provides a significant mental and physical boost. The caffeine and glucose combination is well-supported.

## During Training: What to Practice

### Session type → Fueling approach

| Session | Fueling |
|---|---|
| Swim <90 min | Water only, before and after |
| Bike <75 min | Water only |
| Bike 75–120 min | 1 gel + water |
| Bike 120–180 min | 60 g carbs/hr. Begin real fueling practice. |
| Bike 180+ min | Full race fueling practice: 70–80 g/hr |
| Run <60 min | Water only |
| Run 60–90 min | 1 gel + water at 45 min |
| Long run 90+ min | Gel every 35–40 min, water |
| Any brick session | Fuel through the bike, take a gel at start of run |

## Hydration

> Dehydration and hyponatraemia (over-hydration) are both risks in Ironman. The goal is balanced hydration, not maximum fluid intake.

**Simple rule:** drink to thirst on the bike, aim for ~500ml/hr as a baseline in mild weather. In heat (>25°C), increase to 700–800ml/hr and ensure sodium intake.

**Sweat rate test (do in training):**
1. Weigh yourself before a 60-min ride (naked, after toilet)
2. Ride for 60 min, drink nothing or note exact intake
3. Weigh yourself after (naked)
4. Weight loss = sweat loss (1 kg ≈ 1 litre)
5. This is your hourly sweat rate

**Signs of dehydration:** dark urine, headache, elevated HR at normal effort, cramps

**Signs of hyponatraemia (over-hydration):** nausea, bloating, clear urine, confusion (more common in slow finishers who drink too much water)

## Recovery Nutrition *(important but often neglected)*

Within **30 minutes** of finishing any session >90 min:
- **Carbohydrate:** 1–1.2 g per kg body weight = 90–110 g for you
- **Protein:** 20–30 g
- **Fluid:** enough to restore pre-session weight

**Simple recovery meal examples:**
- 500ml chocolate milk + banana
- Rice + chicken + vegetables
- Yogurt + granola + fruit
- Protein shake + 2 slices toast with jam

> [!TIP]
> The 30-minute window matters most after very hard sessions (long bricks, key intervals). After easy sessions, just eat your normal meal.

## Body Composition Note

At 91 kg with some body fat around the midsection: **training volume will naturally influence body composition** without needing to restrict food. Do not attempt to lose weight aggressively during this training plan — under-fuelling endurance training increases injury risk, slows recovery, and impairs performance.

If body composition changes naturally as volume increases over 6–9 months, that's fine. Prioritise performance and adequate fuelling above all else.
