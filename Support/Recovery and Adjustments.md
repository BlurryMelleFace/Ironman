# Recovery & Adjustments — IRONMAN Belgium 2027

> [!NOTE]
> Recovery is not the absence of training — it is when adaptation happens. A plan you can't follow is not a good plan. This document tells you how to be flexible without losing progress.

---

## The Recovery Hierarchy

Not all recovery is equal. In order of importance:

1. **Sleep** — 7–9 hours per night. No training adaptation compensates for chronic sleep debt.
2. **Nutrition timing** — fuel within 30 min of hard sessions (see [[Nutrition and Fueling]])
3. **Easy days truly easy** — Z1/Z2 only on recovery sessions. Moderate days accumulate.
4. **Recovery weeks** — mandatory 3:1 loading cycle
5. **Active recovery** — easy walking, stretching, light swimming

---

## Recovery Weeks

Every 4th week, volume drops ~35–40% with intensity maintained. This is planned into the schedule — it is not a weakness.

**What changes in recovery weeks:**
- Long ride: reduce by 40%
- Long run: reduce by 40%
- Swim: reduce by 35%, keep some drills/quality
- Strength: reduce to 1 light session or skip
- Total sessions: one fewer session than a normal week

**What does NOT change in recovery weeks:**
- Sleep — keep to the same routine
- Nutrition — keep fueling well
- Any brief sharpeners/strides — short quality efforts are fine

> [!TIP]
> You will often feel your best at the END of a recovery week, not the beginning. Feeling flat on day 2 of a recovery week is normal — it means the fatigue is finally clearing.

## How to Handle Missed Sessions

Life happens. Here's how to respond without derailing your plan.

### One missed session

**→ Simply skip it. Do not try to make it up.**

Doubling up the next day adds fatigue and disrupts your body's recovery rhythm. One missed session in a 48-week plan is meaningless. Move on.

### 2–3 missed sessions (e.g., busy work week)

**→ Prioritise your "anchor sessions" and let the rest go.**

**Anchor sessions (in priority order if time is very limited):**
1. 🏊 Long swim or swim with technique focus
2. 🚴 Long ride
3. 🧱 Brick session (if in Phase 2–4)
4. 🏃 Long run
5. Everything else

The mid-week easy sessions are valuable but replaceable. The long weekend sessions are not — they build the durability you need on race day.

### Full week missed (illness, travel, exam stress)

**→ Return to training 70% of normal volume for one week.**

Do not jump back in at full intensity. Your body needs a reintroduction. Use this "return week" as a second recovery week, then resume normal training in the following week.

### Two or more weeks missed

**→ Reassess with the following guide:**

| Weeks missed | Volume on return | Intensity on return |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 weeks | 70% of pre-break | Z2 only for 3 days |
| 2–3 weeks | 60% for first week | Z2 only for 5 days |
| 3+ weeks | Restart 2 phases back | Z2 only for 7–10 days |

## Illness Protocol

### Mild illness (runny nose, sore throat, no fever)

**Above the neck:** You can usually continue training at reduced intensity (Z1–Z2 only, shorter sessions).

Wait 24 hours after symptoms begin. If no fever and you feel reasonably well, a short easy swim or walk is fine.

### Moderate illness (fever, body aches, fatigue)

**Stop training completely.** Training with a fever significantly increases recovery time and risks complications (myocarditis in rare cases if training through viral illness).

- Rest completely until fever-free for 48 hours
- Then follow the "return from break" protocol above
- A week of illness followed by a conservative return costs far less than pushing through and being sick for 3 weeks

### Injury

**Distinguish between pain and discomfort:**
- **Discomfort** (general soreness, muscle fatigue) → continue with care
- **Sharp pain, joint pain, swelling, limping** → stop that activity, seek assessment

**Injury management principles:**
- Maintain fitness in other disciplines while injured (e.g., pool running or swimming if you can't run)
- See a physiotherapist early — a €60 assessment now can prevent a 4-week training gap
- Most running injuries respond well to load reduction + strength work, not complete rest

## Signs You Need an Unplanned Recovery Day

Take an extra rest day if 3 or more of the following are true:

- [ ] Resting heart rate is >8 bpm above your normal in the morning
- [ ] Your mood is noticeably low, irritable, or flat
- [ ] Sleep has been poor for 2+ consecutive nights
- [ ] Training feels harder than it should at the same intensity
- [ ] You have muscle soreness that didn't resolve after 48 hours
- [ ] You've been sick or feel you're getting sick

**Action:** Take the day off, eat well, sleep extra, go for a walk. One day off preserves many future training days.

## Exam Period Adjustments (April–July 2027)

This period already has reduced volume built in (Phase 3: 6–8 h/week). If you need to reduce further:

### If exam pressure increases and training feels impossible:

**Minimum effective dose to maintain fitness:**
- 2 swims per week (45 min each)
- 1 long ride per week (2:30–3:00 h)
- 0 structured runs (replace with easy walks/jogs if desired)
- Strength: skip entirely if needed

This is the bare minimum. Two weeks at this level = minimal fitness loss. Three weeks = some loss but recoverable in Phase 4.

### The most important thing during exam period:

**Sleep beats training.** If you have to choose between 7 hours of sleep + no training, or 5 hours of sleep + a training session — sleep every time.

## Recovery Modalities

### Definitely useful
- **Sleep** — irreplaceable
- **Easy walking** — promotes blood flow without fatigue
- **Light swimming** — the most effective active recovery for triathletes
- **Foam rolling / self-massage** — reduces perceived soreness (evidence is moderate but subjectively helpful)
- **Nutrition** — see [[Nutrition and Fueling]]

### Probably useful (use if you enjoy them, don't stress if not)
- Cold water immersion (cold shower, cold bath) — some evidence for reducing inflammation
- Compression socks after hard sessions
- Stretching — useful for hip flexors, calves, and thoracic spine

### Not worth spending money on
- Expensive recovery supplements (beyond basic protein and carbs)
- Compression boots (useful but equivalent to elevation + rest)
- Cryotherapy chambers (minimal evidence beyond cold water immersion)

## Overtraining — Know the Warning Signs

True overtraining (Overtraining Syndrome, OTS) is rare but real. Functional overreaching (short-term excessive fatigue) is more common and fully reversible.

**Warning signs of overreaching:**
- Performance declining despite consistent training
- Persistent fatigue not resolved by a rest day
- Getting ill frequently (>2 colds in 2 months)
- Loss of motivation for training (not normal exam/life stress)
- Resting HR chronically elevated

**Response:** Full rest for 5–7 days, then a very gradual return. If symptoms persist beyond 2 weeks despite rest, consult a sports doctor.
